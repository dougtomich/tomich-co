🏋️ Must-Have Strength Work

Running only moves you forward. Over a long race, the muscles that keep you steady side to side get tired first, and that's when legs turn to slop. This is the routine I use to stay upright late in a race.

By Doug Tomich · Updated November 2026

The day for side-to-side work. These are the stabilizers that quit first in a long race.

1

Lateral Lunges

3 × 10 each side

Stand with your feet wide. Shift your weight onto one leg and sit your hips back like you're lowering into a chair, keeping the other leg straight. Push back to center and switch sides. In F3 we call it a Willie Mays Hayes.

Tip: Keep your chest up and your heel down. Go only as deep as you can without your knee caving in.

2

Banded Lateral Walks

3 × 15 steps each way

Put the band around your legs just above your knees (around the ankles is harder). Get into a quarter squat and take slow steps sideways, keeping tension on the band the whole time. Go 15 steps one way, then 15 back.

Tip: Don't let your feet snap together. Stay low, and you should feel it in the side of your hip.

3

Banded Clamshells

3 × 15 each side

Lie on your side with the band above your knees, knees bent and heels together. Keeping your feet touching, open your top knee like a clamshell, pause, then lower it slowly. This works the glute medius, the muscle that stops your pelvis from dropping on every stride.

Tip: Don't roll your hips backward to cheat the rep. Keep it small and controlled.

4

Copenhagen Plank

3 × 20 sec each side

Side plank on your forearm with your top leg resting on a bench or couch and your bottom leg hanging free. Lift your hips so your body is in a straight line, then hold. It's the best inner-thigh exercise I've found, and almost nobody trains it.

Tip: Start with your knee on the bench instead of your foot. It's much easier, and it still counts.

The basics: legs, glutes, calves, and a core that holds you upright when you're tired.

1

Bodyweight Squats

3 × 15

Feet about shoulder width apart, toes turned slightly out. Sit your hips back and down until your thighs are about parallel to the floor, then drive back up through your whole foot.

Tip: Push your knees out over your toes. If 15 gets easy, hold a sandbag or slow down the lowering.

2

Single-Leg Glute Bridge

3 × 12 each side

Lie on your back, one knee bent with that foot flat, the other leg straight in the air. Drive through your heel and lift your hips until your body is straight from shoulder to knee. Squeeze at the top, then lower slowly. Working one leg at a time is how your legs work when you run.

Tip: Keep your hips level. If one side drops, that's the side that needs the work.

3

Slow Calf Raises

3 × 15

Stand on the edge of a step. Rise up onto your toes, then take a slow 3-count to lower your heels below the step. That slow lowering is what builds ankle and Achilles durability.

Tip: Hold a wall for balance. Once both legs get easy, do them one leg at a time.

4

Plank

3 × 45 sec

Forearms on the ground, elbows under your shoulders, body in a straight line from head to heels. Brace your stomach like you're about to get punched, and breathe.

Tip: Squeeze your glutes. If your hips sag or your lower back starts to ache, end the set.

My buddy couldn't walk for three weeks after his first 100-miler. After he added these five, he went for a walk the day after his second.

1

Copenhagen Plank

3 × 20 sec each side

Side plank with your top leg on a bench and your hips lifted. Works the inner thighs. They're the muscles you don't notice until mile 60.

Tip: A bent knee on the bench is fine to start.

2

Lateral Lunges

3 × 10 each side

Wide stance, shift onto one leg, and sit your hips back. Running only goes forward, and your hips need to handle sideways too.

Tip: Sit back into the hip and keep your chest up.

3

Banded Clamshells

3 × 15 each side

Band above your knees, lying on your side, open and close your top knee. When your glute medius is weak, your pelvis drops and your knees pay for it.

Tip: Don't roll your hips back.

4

Single-Leg Glute Bridge

3 × 12 each side

One foot planted, the other leg up, hips driving to the ceiling. Each side works on its own, the way it does when you're running.

Tip: Squeeze at the top and lower slowly.

5

Slow Calf Raises

3 × 15

Off the edge of a step, up quick and down slow. This is armor for your ankles.

Tip: Take a 3-count on the way down.

How I fit it in

I do strength on days I'm not doing a hard run, usually a Day 1, Day 2, Day 3 rotation through the week. Race week, I cut it back to one set of each, or skip it entirely. The goal is to show up at the start line durable, not sore.
